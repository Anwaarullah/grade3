# Unit 12 More fractions (+ Project 4) - syllabus notes (paraphrased; do not copy book text)

Source: Learner's Book 3, printed pages 183-199 (scan images 185-201).

**Page offset note:** the offset is **+2, not +4** (image 185 = printed 183, image 201 = printed 199). Image 202 is printed 200 (start of Unit 13). Printed page numbers were checked in the corners.

**Learner flag:** 12.1 is division in disguise (a fraction of a number = divide by the denominator, then multiply by the numerator for 3/4). The child is weak at x and /, so fraction-of-a-number work should be done by sharing objects into equal groups first, with the division sentence shown afterwards. 12.2 and 12.3 are mostly visual (number lines, strips) and suit him better.

### 12.1 Fractions of numbers (WB p.165-169)

Key-word box on p.165: denominator, diagram, dividing line, non-unit fraction, numerator, unit fraction (same as the textbook).

- **p.165 Q1 (Focus)** - A 20 cm fraction strip is being split into quarters; say at which cm mark to put 1/4, 2/4 and 3/4. Answers: a 5 cm, b 10 cm, c 15 cm. (Textbook used a 15 cm strip in thirds - different length and denominator.)
- **p.165 Q2 (Focus)** - A loose scatter of 15 cubes (counted: rows of 4, 5 and 6). a Ring 1/3 of them -> ring 5 cubes. b Say what fraction is NOT ringed -> 2/3 (10/15 also correct). Note: cubes are scattered, not in tidy rows, so the child has to count carefully before sharing.
- **p.166 Q3 (Focus)** - Table: find 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/10, 3/4 of 12. The 1/10 cell is pre-printed with an X (can't be done). Answers: 6, 4, 3, X, 9.
- **p.166 Q4 (Focus)** - Find a unit fraction and write the matching division. a 1/2 of 6 = 3; 6 / 2 = 3. b 1/4 of 16 = 4; 16 / 4 = 4.
- **p.166 Q5 (Focus)** - A chocolate bar made of 3 separate fingers is shared equally by 4 children; draw a diagram. Answer: each child gets 3/4 of a finger (cut each finger into quarters, 12 quarters / 4 children = 3 quarters each). Equivalent to "3 / 4 = 3/4". This is a fraction AS a division answer, harder than the textbook sandwich question (2 sandwiches -> 2 quarters = 1/2 each).
- **p.166 Q6 (Practice)** - A strip 1 metre long; mark the quarters in cm. Answers: 1/4 at 25 cm, 2/4 at 50 cm, 3/4 at 75 cm. Needs 1 m = 100 cm.
- **p.167 Q7 (Practice)** - Picture of 30 small cubes, 3 of them inside a drawn ring (counted on a zoomed crop: 3 inside, 27 outside). a Fraction ringed -> 3/30 = 1/10. b Fraction not ringed -> 27/30 = 9/10. Child must count 30 objects and recognise 3 out of 30 as one tenth (30 makes 10 groups of 3, and one group is ringed).
- **p.167 Q8 (Practice)** - Whole is blank; only the 3/4 cell is given (21). Find the whole and fill the rest. Working: 3/4 = 21 so 1/4 = 7, whole = 28. Row: 1/2 = 14, 1/3 = not possible (28 does not share into 3; child should write X), 1/4 = 7, 1/10 = not possible (X), 3/4 = 21. The X cells are NOT pre-printed here, so the child must decide on their own which cells can't be done. Working back from a NON-unit fraction is not in the textbook (textbook Q6 gave 1/3 = 9).
- **p.167 Q9 (Practice)** - Missing whole, then the matching division. a 1/4 of [48] = 12; 48 / 4 = 12. b 1/2 of [14] = 7; 14 / 2 = 7. (Inverse: multiply the part by the denominator: 12 x 4, 7 x 2.)
- **p.168 Q10 (Practice)** - Pack of 4 tomatoes, 2 are used: fraction used = 2/4 = 1/2.
- **p.168 Q11 (Challenge)** - a A strip has 1/5 marked at 9 cm; how long is the strip? -> 45 cm (9 x 5). b Where should 1/3 go on that strip? -> 15 cm (45 / 3).

- **p.169 Q13 (Challenge)** - 24 marbles fill 3/4 of a bag; how many in a full bag? -> 1/4 = 24 / 3 = 8, whole = 4 x 8 = 32.

Scan note: the printed line measures about 9 cm on the scan by my rough pixel estimate (page scale unknown) - check with a real ruler on the physical book; if short, mark by proportion.
- **p.172 Q6 (Practice)** - Name fractions between 1/4 and 1/2. Open answer: e.g. 1/3, 2/5, 3/10, 4/10, 3/8. (1/4 and 1/2 themselves don't count.)

Answer: Sofia's - the equal piece is a third of hers (3 pieces make her ribbon) but a quarter of Zara's (4 pieces make Zara's). ### 12.3 Calculating with fractions (WB p.175-178)

Key-word box p.175: original, reduced.

- **p.175 Q1 (Focus)** - A tenths strip with 3 light and 2 dark tenths bracketed together; write the addition. Answer: 3/10 + 2/10 = 5/10 (= 1/2).
- **p.175 Q2 (Focus, reasoning icon)** - Fill the numerators in ?/5 + ?/5 = ?/5; find three different solutions (a, b, c). Any three valid, e.g. 1/5 + 1/5 = 2/5; 1/5 + 2/5 = 3/5; 2/5 + 2/5 = 4/5; also 1/5 + 3/5 = 4/5, 1/5 + 4/5 = 5/5, 2/5 + 3/5 = 5/5 (and with 0 if allowed). Totals must stay at or below 5/5 since nothing beyond one whole is taught. Accept turn-arounds as different only if the parent wishes (book does not say).
- **p.175 Q3 (Focus)** - A tenths strip: 8/10 bracketed above, of which the last 5 tenths are crossed out; 3/10 bracketed below. Write the subtraction: 8/10 - 5/10 = 3/10.
- **p.176 Q4 (Focus)** - A TV programme lasts 1/3 of an hour; how many minutes? -> 60 / 3 = 20 minutes. Needs 1 hour = 60 minutes (time/measures; not in the 12.3 textbook pages).
- **p.176 Q5 (Practice)** - Estimate first (ring < 1/2, = 1/2 or > 1/2), then calculate. a 2/3 + 1/3 = 3/3 = 1 (> 1/2). b 1 - 5/5 = 0 (< 1/2). c 1/10 + ?/10 = ?/10 - both the second numerator and the answer are blank, so the child chooses; e.g. 1/10 + 3/10 = 4/10 (< 1/2), 1/10 + 4/10 = 5/10 (= 1/2), 1/10 + 6/10 = 7/10 (> 1/2). The estimate must match the child's own choice. d 1/2 - 1/4 = 1/4 (< 1/2) - DIFFERENT denominators (needs 1/2 = 2/4).
- **p.177 Q6 (Practice, reasoning icon)** - Find all solutions to 1 - ?/3 = ?/3. Answers (4): 1 - 0/3 = 3/3; 1 - 1/3 = 2/3; 1 - 2/3 = 1/3; 1 - 3/3 = 0/3.
- **p.177 Q7 (Practice)** - A $30 T-shirt is reduced by 1/3; what does she pay? -> 1/3 of 30 = 10; 30 - 10 = $20. (Textbook used 1/10 and 1/4 reductions; this is 1/3.)
- **p.177 Q8 (Challenge)** - 200 ml orange + 200 ml pineapple + 200 ml lemonade; what fraction of a litre is the drink? -> 600 ml = 6/10 of a litre (= 3/5). Needs 1 litre = 1000 ml and thinking of 100 ml as 1/10 of a litre - not taught in the textbook unit.
- **p.178 Q9 (Challenge, reasoning icon)** - Check each calculation with strips; tick if right, correct if wrong. a 5/10 + 4/10 = 9/10 -> correct (tick). b 5/5 - 1/5 = 3/5 -> wrong, should be 4/5. c 3/4 - 1/4 = 1/2 -> correct (2/4 = 1/2), a trap because the answer is written in a different denominator. d 3/3 - 0/3 = 0/3 -> wrong, should be 3/3 (= 1). e 10/10 - 3/10 = 1/10 -> wrong, should be 7/10.
- **p.178 Q10 (Challenge)** - 20 m of fabric, 9/10 used; how much is left? -> 1/10 of 20 = 2, so 2 m left (9/10 = 18 m used). Smart route: left = 1 - 9/10 = 1/10. Two-step; answer is a length in metres.

### Hidden prerequisites and traps

- **Measures conversions not taught in Unit 12 textbook pages:** 1 m = 100 cm (p.166 Q6), 1 hour = 60 minutes (p.176 Q4), 1 litre = 1000 ml with 100 ml as a tenth (p.177 Q8), metres of fabric (p.178 Q10). The child must recall these before any fraction work.
- **Ruler skill:** p.172 Q5 needs marking a 10 cm line at 5, 7, 9 cm; p.165/166/168 strips need cm reasoning. Printed line length unverified on the scan.
- **Different strip lengths and denominators from the textbook:** 20 cm in quarters, 100 cm in quarters, a 45 cm strip found from 1/5 = 9 cm, then thirds (textbook: 15 cm thirds, 50 cm from tenths).
- **Working back from a non-unit fraction** (p.167 Q8: 3/4 = 21 -> 28; p.169 Q13: 3/4 = 24 -> 32) and from the part LEFT (p.169 Q14: 2/3 left = 18 -> 27). Textbook only works back from unit fractions (1/3 = 4, 1/3 = 9). Heavy x/÷ load: 21/3, 24/3, 18/2, 7x4, 8x4, 9x3.
- **Child must decide "can't be done" alone:** p.167 Q8 has no pre-printed X; 1/3 and 1/10 of 28 are not whole numbers.
- **Fraction as the result of sharing** (p.166 Q5: 3 bars between 4 children = 3/4 each). Textbook only had a result of 2 quarters.
- **Writing a fraction from a count, including simplifying:** 2/4 -> 1/2, 9/12 -> 3/4, 10/15 -> 2/3, 3/30 -> 1/10. The textbook did not teach simplifying; accept unsimplified answers but know the simple forms. 3/30 = 1/10 needs seeing 10 groups of 3.
- **Counting large scattered sets accurately:** 15 cubes (p.165), 30 cubes (p.167), 12 nails (p.169).
- **Order direction switches:** smallest-to-largest (p.171 Q4a) vs largest-to-smallest (p.171 Q4b, p.174 Q10). Easy to reverse.
- **Comparing fractions OF NUMBERS with signs** (p.174 Q11): needs halves/quarters/thirds/tenths of 18, 32, 60, 100, 40 - numbers bigger than textbook (max 40 there). Includes an "=" case.
- **Same-numerator, different denominator reasoning in context:** p.168 Q12 and p.172 Q7 (if equal-length pieces are 1/4 vs 1/3, the 1/3 owner has the shorter ribbon). This reverses the usual "1/3 > 1/4" intuition and is a strong trap. p.172 Q7 picture does not match the text (both bars equal length, marks in the same place) - ambiguous.
- **Different denominators in add/subtract:** p.176 Q5d (1/2 - 1/4) and answer form 1/2 in p.178 Q9c. Outside the "same denominator" objective.
- **Zero numerators:** 1 - 5/5 = 0, 1 - 3/3 = 0/3, 1 - 0/3 = 3/3, 3/3 - 0/3 (p.176 Q5b, p.177 Q6, p.178 Q9d). Child may think 0/3 is not allowed or that subtracting 0 makes 0.
- **"1" written as a whole number in subtractions** (1 - 5/5, 1 - ?/3) - must rewrite 1 as 5/5 or 3/3.
- **Open-ended answers with blank numerators** (p.175 Q2, p.176 Q5c, p.177 Q6, p.170 Q2, p.172 Q6, p.174 Q12): the child must generate, not just compute. p.176 Q5c's estimate depends on the child's own choice.
- **Error-spotting** (p.178 Q9): judge and correct given calculations.
- **Money:** $2 (p.173 Q8b; comparison only) and a 1/3 reduction of $30 (p.177 Q7) - textbook did 1/10 and 1/4 reductions.
- **No equivalent-chain practice in the workbook** - textbook p.191-192 chains (and 9/27) have no workbook support.
- No misprints found; all printed page numbers matched the filenames.

## Proposed activities

Workbook pages are added to each `book` field; "Workbook additions" lists extra question types, mistake tags and tips taken from the workbook.

### 1. `fraction-share-out` - Share It Out 🍪
- **section:** 12.1
- **summary:** Find a fraction of a number by dealing objects into equal groups, then see the matching division.
- **book:** Textbook p.184-187 (intro, Ex 12.1 Q3, Q5, Q7-10) · Workbook p.165 Q2, p.166 Q3-5, p.167 Q7 and Q9, p.168 Q10, p.169 Q15
- **Learn screens:**
  1. 12 cookies and 3 plates. Bolt asks for 1/3 of 12. The child clicks "deal" until all cookies are shared; one plate glows: 1/3 of 12 = 4. The sentence 12 / 3 = 4 slides in underneath.
  2. 3/4 of 20: 20 buttons dealt into 4 jars (5 each). The child clicks three jars to choose them; they count 5, 10, 15. Message: find one part, then take as many parts as the top number says.
  3. When it won't share: 20 dealt into 3 plates leaves 2 over. Bolt says 1/3 of 20 isn't a whole number - that box gets a "can't share" stamp.
  4. Pattern: 1/4 of 4, 8, 12, 16 ... with jars filling one more each row - the 4 times table running backwards.
- **Practice (count 9):**
  - Level 1: unit fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/10 of 4-20; objects shown, child deals and types the answer.
  - Level 2: 1/3, 1/5, 1/10 of numbers up to 40 (book: 1/2 of 24, 1/4 of 40); type the answer and complete "__ / __ = __".
  - Level 3: 2/3, 3/4 of 12-40 (book: 3/4 of 20), or "is 1/3 of 20 possible?" yes/no; ring 1/10 of 20 marbles then say what fraction is not ringed.
- **Mistake tags:** Divided by the top number; Found one part only for 3/4; Forced an answer that doesn't share; Multiplied instead of divided.
- **Workbook additions:**
  - "What fraction is ringed / not ringed?" for scattered sets of 15 and 30 objects (1/3 and 2/3; 3 of 30 = 1/10 and 9/10), with a helper that snaps objects into equal rows.
  - "What fraction is used?" from a pack (2 of 4 tomatoes, 9 of 12 nails): accept 2/4 or 1/2, 9/12 or 3/4, then show the simpler form on a strip.
  - Fraction table for 12 where the child decides which cell gets the "can't share" stamp (1/10 of 12); missing whole boxes (1/4 of __ = 12 -> 48).
  - Level 3: 3 chocolate fingers shared by 4 children: cut each into quarters and deal 12 quarters, so each gets 3/4 of a finger.
  - Extra mistake tags: Miscounted a scattered set; Gave a number (27) instead of a fraction; Shared whole bars instead of cutting them.
  - Extra parent tips: "Count the set twice before sharing." / "2 out of 4 is 2/4 - can you see it is also a half?"
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: Know that finding 1/3 of a number means sharing it into 3 equal groups.
  - Say: "How many groups does the bottom number tell us?" / "How many are in one group?" / "For 3/4, how many groups do we take?"
  - Watch for: using the top number to share; stopping at one group for 3/4.
  - Away from screen: share 12 grapes between 3 bowls, then say "a third of 12 is 4".

### 2. `whole-from-part` - Find the Whole 🧩
- **section:** 12.1
- **summary:** Work backwards from a part (one third is 4 cars) to the whole, and mark fractions on a measured strip.
- **book:** Textbook p.185-186 (Ex 12.1 Q1, Q2, Q4, Q6, Think like a mathematician) · Workbook p.165 Q1, p.166 Q6, p.167 Q8, p.168 Q11-12, p.169 Q13-14
- **Learn screens:**
  1. A tray with 4 toy cars labelled "1/3 of my cars". Bolt shows two more empty trays of the same size; the child drags cars to fill them (copies of the first tray). Whole = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.
  2. A 15 cm paper strip on a ruler. The child clicks to fold it into 3 equal parts; folds land at 5 cm and 10 cm.
  3. A strip with tenth marks every 5 cm: the child counts on 5, 10 ... and finds the end at 50 cm.
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: one part shown (2-5 objects) is 1/2 or 1/3 of the set; drag copies to find the whole.
  - Level 2: strips of 6-30 cm split into halves, thirds, quarters or fifths; click the cm mark for a given fraction (book: 1/3 and 2/3 of 15 cm).
  - Level 3: "1/3 is 9, what is the whole?" (book: 27); tenth marks every 2, 3, 4 or 5 cm - find the whole length.
- **Mistake tags:** Gave the part as the whole; Added instead of copying the part; Wrong fold position; Counted marks not spaces.
- **Workbook additions (harder than the textbook):**
  - Work back from a non-unit fraction: 3/4 of a bag is 24 marbles -> share 24 into 3 trays (8), add a 4th tray -> 32; 3/4 = 21 -> 28, then decide alone which table cells can't be done (1/3 and 1/10 of 28).
  - "Part left" problem: 1/3 of the grapes eaten, 18 left -> 18 is 2 parts of 9 -> 27.
  - Strips: 20 cm in quarters (5, 10, 15 cm); 1 m = 100 cm in quarters (25, 50, 75 cm) - needs 1 m = 100 cm; 1/5 marked at 9 cm -> strip 45 cm, then 1/3 at 15 cm.
  - Torn strips: two pieces each labelled 1/3 - the strip with the shorter third is the shorter strip.
  - Extra mistake tags: Shared by 4 instead of 3 for 3/4; Answered with the part left (18); Used 1 m = 10 cm.
  - Extra parent tips: "3/4 means 3 trays - share into 3 first, then add one more tray." / "The grapes left are 2 of the 3 parts."
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: If you know one equal part, you can build the whole by repeating that part.
  - Say: "How many parts like this make the whole?" / "Can you add the same again, and again?" / "Where would the first fold be?"
  - Watch for: answering with the part; counting tick marks instead of the gaps between them.
  - Away from screen: fold a paper strip into thirds and measure where the folds are with a ruler.

### 3. `fraction-line-up` - Fraction Line-Up 📏
- **section:** 12.2
- **summary:** Place fractions on a 0-1 number line and compare them with <, > and = using a fraction wall.
- **book:** Textbook p.188-190 (Ex 12.2 Q1-6), Check your progress Q1-2 p.197 · Workbook p.170 Q1-3, p.171 Q4, p.172 Q5-7, p.173 Q8-9, p.174 Q10-12
- **Learn screens:**
  1. A fraction wall (one whole, halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, tenths). The child clicks 1/4 and 1/5; drop lines show 1/5 is shorter. Bolt: more pieces means smaller pieces.
  2. A 0-1 line. The child picks "quarters" and faint ticks appear; drag 1/4, 2/4, 3/4 onto them. 2/4 lands exactly on the half mark.
  3. Signs: two fractions with a gap. The child drags the longer strip up - the sign's open mouth faces the bigger one; drag <, > or =.
  4. Better deal: 1/3 or 1/4 of the same chocolate bar of 12 squares - the child picks, Bolt shows 4 squares vs 3.
- **Practice (count 9):**
  - Level 1: compare two unit fractions (denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 10) with the wall visible; choose <, >.
  - Level 2: same-denominator pairs (2/5 vs 1/5, 7/10 vs 9/10) and equivalence (2/4 vs 1/2, 5/10 vs 1/2, 4/4 vs 10/10); drag to a 0-1 line or choose a sign.
  - Level 3: order five unit fractions smallest to largest by dragging cards (book: 1/2, 1/10, 1/4, 1/5, 1/3); or place 1/3, 2/3 and 2/10 on one line; or "1/3 or 1/4 of $100?".
- **Mistake tags:** Thinks bigger bottom number is bigger; Sign the wrong way round; Equal fractions marked as different; Uneven spacing on the line.
- **Workbook additions:**
  - Name any fraction between 0 and 1/4, or between 1/4 and 1/2, or bigger than 1/3 (pick from the wall; any answer in range is right).
  - Order smallest-to-largest AND largest-to-smallest (the workbook switches direction between parts), including tenths 3/10-10/10; show a direction arrow.
  - "Which gives more?" in context: 1/3 or 1/10 of a cake, 2/4 or 3/4 of $2, 5/5 or 1/5 of a pizza.
  - Mark 5/10, 7/10, 9/10 on a 10 cm line (1 cm per tenth) with the on-screen ruler.
  - Ribbon trap: the same-length piece is 1/4 of one ribbon and 1/3 of another - which whole is shorter? (the 1/3 one). Build both wholes from copies of the piece; the workbook picture draws both ribbons the same length, so do not rely on it.
  - Level 3 (optional): compare fractions OF numbers with <, >, = (1/2 of 18 vs 1/4 of 32; 1/10 of 60 = 1/3 of 18); show both amounts with share-out trays so only the sign is the child's job.
  - Extra mistake tags: Ordered in the wrong direction; Fraction outside the range; Bigger piece means bigger whole (ribbon trap).
  - Extra parent tip: "Smallest first or largest first? Check the arrow."
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: Compare and order fractions using a number line or fraction strips and the signs <, > and =.
  - Say: "Which piece is longer?" / "If a pizza is cut into more slices, are the slices bigger or smaller?" / "Where is one half on this line?"
  - Watch for: 1/10 > 1/2 because 10 > 2; mixing up < and >.
  - Away from screen: cut two same-size paper strips, one into 4 and one into 5 pieces, and lay a quarter next to a fifth.

### 4. `equivalent-wall` - Equivalent Fraction Wall 🧱
- **section:** 12.2
- **summary:** Build chains of equivalent fractions on a fraction wall and spot the times-table pattern.
- **book:** Textbook p.191-192 (Ex 12.2 Q7-9, Zara reflection, Tip: fraction wall) · Workbook p.173 Q9b, p.178 Q9c (no chain practice in the workbook)
- **Learn screens:**
  1. Half a strip is shaded. The child drags quarter pieces to cover the same length: 2 quarters. Then eighths: 4 eighths. A chain 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8 builds.
  2. Pattern table: tops 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and bottoms 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 light up as the 2x table; Bolt says "both go up in steps".
  3. 1/4 chain: the bottoms count in 4s (4, 8, 12, 16, 20) as the child adds rows to the wall.
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: cover a shaded half or quarter with smaller pieces; type how many pieces (2/4, 5/10, 2/8).
  - Level 2: fill the next term in a chain (1/2 = 2/4 = __/6; 1/10 = 2/20 = __/30; 1/4 = __/8) using a count-in-steps strip.
  - Level 3: fill two missing terms in a chain; or "which unit fraction equals 9/27?" (choose from 1/2, 1/3, 1/9) with a 27-square bar split into 9s.
- **Mistake tags:** Added the same number to top and bottom; Changed only one number; Lost the step pattern; Picked the wrong unit fraction.
- **Workbook additions:** the workbook has no equivalent-chain practice, so this activity stays textbook-led and lower priority. Add "same amount?" matching of the simplified answers the workbook expects: 2/4 = 1/2, 9/12 = 3/4, 10/15 = 2/3, 3/30 = 1/10, 2/10 = 1/5, 6/10 = 3/5 (shown on strips or sets). Extra mistake tag: Thinks a right answer in another form is wrong (2/4 vs 1/2). Parent tip: "Lay the strips side by side - same length means same amount."
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: See that fractions like 1/2, 2/4 and 5/10 are the same amount, and that the numbers follow times tables.
  - Say: "Do these pieces cover exactly the same length?" / "What are the bottom numbers counting in?" / "If the bottom doubles, what happens to the top?"
  - Watch for: 1/2 = 2/3 = 3/4 (adding 1 each time).
  - Away from screen: fold a sheet of paper in half, then in half again, and count how many quarters make the half.

### 5. `fraction-strip-sums` - Fraction Strip Sums ➕
- **section:** 12.3
- **summary:** Estimate (more or less than a half?) then add and subtract same-denominator fractions on strips.
- **book:** Textbook p.192-195 (Ex 12.3 Q1-6, Think like a mathematician), Check your progress Q3 p.198 · Workbook p.175 Q1-3, p.176 Q5, p.177 Q6, p.178 Q9
- **Learn screens:**
  1. An empty tenths strip with a gold half-way line. The child drags 4 orange tenths, then 3 blue tenths. The strip reads 7/10. Bolt: only the top number changes - the pieces are still tenths (so not 7/20 or 7/70).
  2. Estimate before you add: three buttons (< 1/2, = 1/2, > 1/2). The strip shows where the total lands relative to the gold line.
  3. Subtracting: a strip shows 4/5; the child clicks 3 pieces to cross them out; 1/5 is left.
  4. One whole minus: a full strip is 10/10. Take away 3/10 to leave 7/10.
- **Practice (count 10):**
  - Level 1: add two same-denominator fractions (thirds, quarters, fifths) with total up to 1; strip always shown.
  - Level 2: estimate first (< 1/2, = 1/2, > 1/2), then add or subtract tenths and fifths (book: 2/10 + 3/10, 3/5 - 2/5, 8/10 - 6/10).
  - Level 3: subtract from 1 (1 - 3/4, 1 - 3/10, 1 - 1/4), or find all pairs of quarters/fifths that make 1 by dragging pieces in two colours.
- **Mistake tags:** Added the bottom numbers; Forgot 1 is a whole strip; Estimate on the wrong side of a half; Missed a pair that makes 1.
- **Workbook additions:**
  - Read strips: 3/10 + 2/10, and 8/10 - 5/10 with crossed-out tenths.
  - Open questions: three different ways to make ?/5 + ?/5 (total at most 5/5); all four answers to 1 - ?/3 = ?/3 including 0/3 and 3/3; "choose your own" 1/10 + ?/10 and estimate.
  - Zero results and zero numerators: 1 - 5/5 = 0; 3/3 - 0/3 = 3/3.
  - Spot the mistake: tick or correct five given sums (e.g. 5/5 - 1/5 = 3/5 is wrong; 3/4 - 1/4 = 1/2 is right).
  - Stretch only: 1/2 - 1/4 with a halves strip over a quarters strip (different denominators).
  - Extra mistake tags: Thinks taking away 0 leaves 0; Didn't accept 0/3 or 3/3; Marked a right answer wrong because it was simplified.
  - Extra parent tip: "Taking away nothing leaves everything: 3/3 - 0/3 is still 3/3."
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator, staying within one whole, and estimate first.
  - Say: "What size are the pieces? Does that change?" / "Is it more than half the strip or less?" / "How many tenths make a whole strip?"
  - Watch for: 4/10 + 3/10 = 7/20; treating 1 - 3/4 as 'can't do'.
  - Away from screen: use a chocolate bar or egg box: eat (or remove) some pieces and say the subtraction in fractions.

### 6. `sale-price-shop` - Sale Price Shop 🏷️
- **section:** 12.3
- **summary:** Find the new price when items are reduced by 1/10 or 1/4 by splitting coin piles into equal parts.
- **book:** Textbook p.195-196 (Worked example 1, Ex 12.3 Q7) · Workbook p.177 Q7, p.178 Q10
- **Learn screens:**
  1. A $30 pair of trousers shown as 30 coins in 10 equal stacks of 3. "Reduce by 1/10": the child clicks one stack to remove it. 30 - 3 = 27; the tag changes to $27.
  2. A $20 jumper reduced by 1/4: 20 coins dealt into 4 piles of 5; one pile is removed: $15.
  3. Check: the discount is not the new price - Bolt shows both numbers on two different tags ("you save $5", "you pay $15").
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: price $4-$20, reduce by 1/2 or 1/4; coins shown; click piles to remove, type new price.
  - Level 2: price $8-$40 reduced by 1/4 or 1/10 (book: $8, $4, $32, $20, and $30 by 1/10).
  - Level 3: two-step "how much do you save and what do you pay?" with prices up to $60 and 1/3, 1/4, 1/5 or 1/10.
- **Mistake tags:** Gave the saving as the new price; Shared into the wrong number of piles; Subtracted the fraction's number (e.g. took $4 off for 1/4).
- **Workbook additions:** $30 reduced by 1/3 -> $20 (30 coins in 3 piles of 10); "how much is left?" for 20 m of fabric with 9/10 used -> 2 m (shortcut: 1/10 is left). Extra mistake tag: Gave the amount used, not the amount left. Parent tip: "If 9 tenths are used, only 1 tenth is left."
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: Know that "reduced by 1/4" means find a quarter of the price and take it away.
  - Say: "How many equal piles does 1/4 need?" / "Which pile do we take away?" / "What do we pay now, and how much did we save?"
  - Watch for: taking $4 off for a quarter; answering with the saving.
  - Away from screen: play shop with toy price tags and real coins; halve or quarter the price in a pretend sale.

### 7. `dicey-tenths` - Dicey Tenths 🎲
- **section:** Project 4
- **summary:** A 3 x 3 game against Bolt: place dice numbers as tenths and make a line that adds to one whole.
- **book:** Textbook p.199 (Project 4: Dicey fractions)
- **Learn screens:**
  1. The grid of nine "?/10" boxes. The child rolls an on-screen dice (1-6) and drags the number into a box. Each line shows a tenths bar filling towards 1.
  2. Puzzle (original position, not the book's): a part-filled grid and a roll; the child finds the box that makes a line reach exactly 10/10. Bolt highlights every line that would be completed.
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: one box missing in a line; which dice number makes the line one whole? (two numbers given, totals 4-9 tenths).
  - Level 2: given a roll and a part-filled grid, click the winning box (one winning spot).
  - Level 3: grids with two possible winning spots or none (choose "no winning move"), or play a full game against Bolt.
- **Mistake tags:** Added the denominators; Missed a column or diagonal; Line adds to more than one whole.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: Add three tenths to make one whole (10 tenths), and think ahead in a game.
  - Say: "How many more tenths does this line need?" / "Check the diagonals too." / "Does going first help?"
  - Watch for: adding 10s together; only checking rows.
  - Away from screen: play the game on paper with a real dice, taking turns.

### 8. `fraction-of-measures` - Measure Fractions 🥤
- **section:** 12.1 / 12.3 (workbook-led)
- **summary:** Find fractions of 1 metre, 1 hour and 1 litre - the hidden conversions the workbook expects.
- **book:** Workbook p.166 Q6, p.176 Q4, p.177 Q8, p.178 Q10 (not taught in the Unit 12 textbook pages)
- **Learn screens:**
  1. A metre stick (100 cm) cut into 4 equal pieces: the child clicks the fold points, which land at 25, 50 and 75 cm.
  2. A clock face (60 minutes) shaded in thirds: each third is 20 minutes, counted in 5s round the face.
  3. A 1-litre jug with 10 marks of 100 ml: the child pours three 200 ml cups and reads 600 ml = 6/10 of the jug.
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: 1/2 and 1/4 of 1 m in cm; 1/2 of an hour in minutes.
  - Level 2: 1/3 and 1/4 of an hour (20, 15 min); 1/10 of 1 m (10 cm); 1/2 of a litre (500 ml).
  - Level 3: what fraction of a litre is 600 ml (or 300, 800 ml) made from 100/200 ml cups; 20 m or 30 m of fabric with 9/10 used - how much is left?
- **Mistake tags:** Used 1 m = 10 cm; Used 100 minutes in an hour; Added the ml but forgot to make a fraction; Gave the amount used, not left.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: Use 100 cm in a metre, 60 minutes in an hour and 1000 ml in a litre to find simple fractions of these measures.
  - Say: "How many cm in a metre? minutes in an hour?" / "Share the 60 minutes into 3 equal parts." / "How many 100 ml cups fill the litre jug?"
  - Watch for: 1/4 of an hour as 25 minutes (thinking an hour is 100); writing 600 instead of a fraction.
  - Away from screen: pour 100 ml cups of water into a 1-litre jug and count the tenths.
