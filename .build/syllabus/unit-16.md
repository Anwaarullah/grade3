### What the workbook expects that the textbook does not teach (hidden prerequisites / traps)
- **likely / unlikely as an answer**: the textbook lists "likely" as a key word but only ever asks will / might / won't. Workbook Q1a and the worked example ask for likely vs unlikely, which is a judgement about *how* likely. With red = 2 of 4 (exactly half) the "right" answer is not obvious; accept either with a reason about the 2 red parts.
- **"greater chance" / comparing chances by counting equal parts** (WE1b, Q6, Q9): 1 of 8 vs 1 of 2, 1 of 6 vs 1 of 3. This is fraction thinking (Unit 7/12) and is only lightly touched in the textbook (spinner A vs B).
- **"half the spaces"** (WE1c) assumes the child sees 3 of 6 as half.
- **Unequal spinner reading** (Q4): yellow is a half, red a quarter, green and blue an eighth each; the child must see that "biggest" still only means "might".
- **New words**: "may happen" (Q8, a synonym the textbook never uses), "conjecture" (Q9b), "Not heads" as a results category (Q6).
- **Movement directions on a grid** (Q3: up / left, staying put at an edge) borrow from Unit 15 (position and movement).
- **Writing** is heavy: most Focus/Practice/Challenge items need a written reason or invented sentence. For this learner, let the parent scribe or offer sentence chips.

### Effect on the proposed activities
- `chance-word-sort`: add a likely / unlikely step for "might" cards at Level 3 (WB Q1a, WE1); include the word "may happen" as a synonym chip (WB Q8). Book refs add WB p.229-230 Q1-Q2, p.232 Q5, p.235 Q8.
- `spinner-lab`: add the coin-vs-8-part-spinner comparison and the 6-part vs 3-part star spinners (WB Q6, Q9); add an "Agree with Bolt?" item where Bolt says an unequal spinner *will* land on its biggest colour (WB Q4). Book refs add WB p.229, p.232-236 Q4, Q6, Q9.
- `sweet-jar-chance`: add a two-bag variant (10 of one colour + 1 of another vs 1 + 1) and "write a chance question" with the word chance (WB Q7). Book refs add WB p.234 Q7.
- `crocodile-coin-walk`: add a grid mode (3×3, spinner says up or left, stay put at an edge; how many spins to reach the corner?) (WB Q3). Book refs add WB p.231 Q3.

## Proposed activities

### 1. `chance-word-sort` - Will it happen?
- **icon**: 🎲  **section**: 16.1  **book**: LB p.249 Getting started Q1-Q3; p.250 intro; p.255 Check your progress Q1; WB p.229 WE1, p.230 Q1-Q2, p.232 Q5, p.235 Q8
- **summary**: Sort everyday events into "will happen", "might happen" and "will not happen".
- **Learn screens**
  1. Bolt holds a normal dice: "It will land on 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 — that **will happen**." Then "It will land on 9" — **will not happen**. Then "It will land on 4" — **might happen**. The child clicks each card to flip and hear the word.
  2. A kitchen window shows tomorrow's weather hidden behind a curtain: rain, cloud or sun **might** happen. The child clicks the curtain three times to see different outcomes.
  3. Three bins (green tick / amber question mark / red cross) are introduced; the child drags 3 sample cards with help.
- **Practice (10 questions)**
  - Level 1: 6 clear-cut cards (a cup of water will be wet; a cat will drive the school bus; it will rain at school today) — drag to a bin.
  - Level 2: 8 cards mixing possible/impossible with "possible vs certain" pairs (the sun will set tonight / you will see a rainbow today); a "possible or impossible?" two-bin round first, then three bins.
  - Level 3: 8 judgement cards (eat lunch today, find a coin in the garden, a tree falls down) — the child picks a bin AND one reason from 3 reason chips ("it happens every day", "it can happen but not always", "it can never happen"). Any bin with a matching reason is accepted.
  - Level 3 extra (workbook): for "might" cards on a spinner picture, also choose likely or unlikely (e.g. 1 black part out of 6 → unlikely); "may happen" is shown as another way to say "might happen".
- **Mistake tags**: `might-as-wont` (said won't for something possible), `usual-as-certain`, `unusual-as-impossible`, `reason-mismatch`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Use the three chance phrases correctly and give a reason.
  - Say: "Could it ever happen? Then it can't be 'will not'." / "Does it happen every single time, no matter what?" / "Tell me why you put it there."
  - Watch for: calling unusual things impossible; calling things that happen most days certain.
  - Away from screen: At breakfast each of you says one thing that will, might and won't happen today; check at bedtime.

### 2. `domino-card-draw` - What could I pick?
- **icon**: 🃏  **section**: 16.1  **book**: p.251 Worked example 1 and Q1; p.256 Check your progress Q2-Q3
- **summary**: Look at what is in a bag or face-down row and decide if a rule will, might or won't be met.
- **Learn screens**
  1. Six dominoes face down (Worked example idea): Bolt asks "Will I get exactly 6 spots?" — we can't see, so we can't be sure: **might**. Click to turn them over.
  2. The same dominoes face up with their totals counted in a dot-count strip (the child clicks each half to count; a blank half counts 0). Rule "more than 8": only one domino matches, so **might**.
  3. Cards 1-7: Bolt shows the biggest pair 6 + 7 = 13 on a number line, so a total of 20 **will not** happen.
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: 4-6 visible dominoes with totals 0-12; rules "total is more than 0", "total is 20", "total is 7" — answer will/might/won't.
  - Level 2: number cards 1-5 or 1-7, pick two; targets from 3 to 20 (some impossible above 13); the child may test pairs on a sum strip before answering.
  - Level 3: the child is shown a target and must find all card pairs that make it (e.g. 10 from 1-7: 3+7, 4+6), then choose will/might/won't.
- **Mistake tags**: `miscounted-spots`, `blank-half-not-zero`, `missed-max-total`, `possible-as-certain`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Decide if something is certain, possible or impossible by checking what is actually in the bag.
  - Say: "Is there at least one that works? Is there one that doesn't?" / "What's the biggest total you could ever make?" / "Count the spots on each half, then add."
  - Watch for: answering "might" without checking any match exists; counting dominoes instead of spots.
  - Away from screen: Put 5 number cards in a cup; take turns asking "Will I pull out a number bigger than 3?" and test it.

### 3. `crocodile-coin-walk` - Coin-toss walk
- **icon**: 🪙  **section**: 16.1  **book**: LB p.252 Q2; WB p.231 Q3
- **summary**: Toss a coin to step a counter along a garden path and see that results change each time.
- **Learn screens**
  1. A 10-stone garden path (pond at one end, shed at the other), counter in the middle. Heads = one stone to the pond, tails = one stone to the shed. The child clicks "toss" 10 times.
  2. The finishing stone is coloured. Bolt asks: "If we play again, will we finish on the same stone?" Child chooses will/might/won't.
  3. Replay; the two finishes are compared. A board keeps a dot for every finish over several games (a simple tally/dot plot).
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: given a short list of 3-5 tosses (H, T, H…), move the counter and click where it ends.
  - Level 2: given 10 tosses, predict the finishing stone, then check; questions such as "could you end at the pond end after 10 tosses?" (will/might/won't).
  - Level 3: after 5 replays shown as dots, answer "which stones were most common?" and "could you ever land 6 stones from the start in 5 tosses?" (won't).
- **Mistake tags**: `wrong-direction`, `expects-repeat`, `step-miscount`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: See that a chance game can end differently every time it is played.
  - Say: "Before we toss, which end do you think we'll reach?" / "Did it match? Why might it be different?" / "Could we land in the same place again?"
  - Watch for: certainty that the result will repeat; moving more than one stone per toss.
  - Away from screen: Play the real game on a paper strip with a real coin and compare two games.

### 4. `spinner-lab` - Spinner lab
- **icon**: 🎡  **section**: 16.1  **book**: LB p.253-255 Q3 and Think like a mathematician; WB p.229 WE1, p.232 Q4, p.233-234 Q6, p.235-236 Q9
- **summary**: Predict, spin and tally to see that bigger sections come up more often.
- **Learn screens**
  1. Two spinners: 3 equal parts (1-3) and 8 equal parts (1-8). The child clicks every possible outcome on each to list them.
  2. "Which is more likely to land on 3?" The child counts sections: 3 is one of 3 pieces on A but one of 8 pieces on B. Spin each 5 times and fill a results table.
  3. An unequal spinner (big A, medium E, small B, C, D). The child estimates out of 20 spins with counters that must total 20, then spins 20 times with auto tally marks; a bar view compares estimate and actual.
- **Practice (10 questions)**
  - Level 1: equal spinners with 2-4 sections (colours from home: sock colours, fruit); list all outcomes; "will it land on purple?" when purple is not on it.
  - Level 2: pick the spinner more likely to land on a colour (sections 2-8, equal; or one colour taking 1 to 3 of 4 quarters).
  - Level 3: unequal spinners (halves, quarters, eighths): order outcomes from most to least likely; estimate counts out of 8 or 20 spins; explain what happens if all parts are equal size.
  - Workbook-style items in Levels 2-3: coin vs a spinner with 1 heads part out of 8 (which gives heads more often?); two star spinners (1 of 6 vs 1 of 3) and "Bolt thinks he will get the star more times — agree?"; "Bolt says this spinner WILL land on yellow" when yellow is half (answer: might).
- **Mistake tags**: `more-numbers-more-likely`, `ignores-section-size`, `missing-outcome`, `estimates-not-total`, `biggest-as-certain`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Link the size of a spinner part to how often it comes up, and compare guesses with results.
  - Say: "Which colour has the biggest slice?" / "Out of 20 spins, how many do you think? Do your guesses add to 20?" / "It didn't match exactly — is that OK?"
  - Watch for: thinking a spinner with more numbers makes 3 more likely; saying the biggest colour "will" happen (the workbook tests this); upset when results differ from the guess.
  - Away from screen: Make a paper-clip spinner with one big and two small parts; spin 20 times and tally.

### 5. `sweet-jar-chance` - Sweet jar questions
- **icon**: 🍬  **section**: 16.1  **book**: LB p.257 Check your progress Q4; WB p.234 Q7
- **summary**: Fill a jar with sweets, then answer and invent chance questions about picking one.
- **Learn screens**
  1. The child drags 5 sweets from 3 colour trays into a jar.
  2. Bolt asks three questions about the jar ("Will I pick a sweet?" will; "Will I pick a blue one?" when none are blue — won't; "Will I pick red?" — might) and the child answers each.
  3. Question builder: choose a colour from chips and an answer type; Bolt checks if the question really has that answer.
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: jars of 3-5 sweets in 1-2 colours; answer will/might/won't.
  - Level 2: jars of 5-8 sweets in 3 colours; include "will I pick a sweet that is not green?" style questions.
  - Level 3: build a jar so that a given statement is true (e.g. "picking red will happen" → all red; "picking yellow might happen"); then write one question of each type using chips.
- **Mistake tags**: `colour-missing-as-might`, `one-colour-as-might`, `question-wrong-type`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Use what is in the jar to decide the chance, and make up chance questions.
  - Say: "Is there any of that colour in the jar?" / "Are ALL the sweets that colour?" / "Make me a question whose answer is 'will not happen'."
  - Watch for: answering "might" when a colour is not there; difficulty making questions — model one first.
  - Away from screen: Fill a bag with 5 pieces of fruit or socks and play "ask a chance question" together.

### 6. `venn-property-game` - Venn card game
- **icon**: ⭕  **section**: Project 5  **book**: p.258-259
- **summary**: Flip two number-property cards and try to place a number in every part of a two-circle Venn diagram.
- **Learn screens**
  1. A two-circle Venn on "Numbers" with labels Even and Multiples of 3. A number line 1-20 shows the even numbers and the counting-in-3s numbers lit in two colours. The child drags 2, 3 and 6 into the three regions (2 points!).
  2. Labels Odd and "one more than a 4x-table number": the strip shows 4, 8, 12 … then +1 gives 5, 9, 13 — all odd. The child tries to fill the right-only region and finds nothing works; Bolt explains that this region stays empty (1 point for explaining).
  3. Scoring rules recap: 2 points for filling all three regions, 1 point for a correct reason why one is empty.
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: properties that need no tables (odd, even, less than 10, more than 20, ends in 5); numbers 1-30 given as tiles to drag.
  - Level 2: one table property (multiples of 2, 5 or 10) plus odd/even/size; numbers 1-50; a skip-counting strip is available.
  - Level 3: pairs where a region must be empty (multiples of 10 and even; odd and multiples of 2); the child chooses "all three filled" or "one is empty" and picks the reason.
- **Mistake tags**: `overlap-misplaced`, `outside-fits-one`, `missed-empty-region`, `multiple-recall`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Sort numbers by two rules at once and notice when two rules can never be apart (or never together).
  - Say: "Does this number fit the left label? The right label? Both?" / "Can you find a number for every part?" / "If you can't, tell me why not."
  - Watch for: putting a number in the middle when it fits only one label; guessing multiples — let him skip-count on the strip.
  - Away from screen: Draw two overlapping hoops on paper (or use two belts on the floor) and sort house numbers or dice totals by two rules.
