## Proposed activities

### 1. `fair-share-pizza` — Fair Shares 🍕
- **section**: 7.1
- **summary**: Deal pizza slices out one at a time to friends, then name each person's share as a fraction of the whole pizza.
- **book**: Textbook p.117 opener (oranges and glasses), p.119–120 Q2 · Workbook p.98 Q3 (four quarters make a whole)
- **Learn screens**
  1. *Equal or not?* Two pizzas each cut into 4: one with equal slices, one with uneven slices. The child taps the fair one. Bolt: fractions only work with equal parts.
  2. *Deal it out.* A 10-slice pizza and 5 plates (you and four friends). The child clicks "deal" and the slices go round one at a time until none are left: 2 each.
  3. *Name the share.* One person's 2 slices glow: "2 of 10 equal slices = 2/10 of the pizza". Then 10 plates: 1 slice each = 1/10.
- **Practice (8)**
  - Level 1: tap the shape cut into equal parts (halves, thirds, quarters, fifths) from 3 options; then share a cake cut into 2, 4 or 10 between that many people and name one share (1/2, 1/4, 1/10).
  - Level 2: share 10 slices between 2, 5 or 10 people (dealing), give slices each and the fraction (5/10, 2/10, 1/10); share a tray of 6 or 9 brownies between 3 people (2/6, 3/9 shown as pieces).
  - Level 3: word problems with "you and __ friends" (remember to count yourself); "Who gets more: 1 of 5 slices or 1 of 10 slices of the same pizza?" (compare unit fractions with pictures).
- **Mistake tags**: `forgot-yourself`, `unequal-parts`, `bigger-denominator-bigger-piece`, `numerator-denominator-swap`
- **Parent tips**
  - goal: Share a whole equally and name each share as a fraction of the whole.
  - say: "Are all the pieces the same size?" · "Don't forget to count yourself!" · "How many pieces is the whole pizza cut into? How many does each person get?"
  - watch: Counting "you and four friends" as 4 people. · Thinking 1/10 is bigger than 1/5 because 10 is bigger. · Accepting pieces that are not equal.
  - away from screen: Cut a sandwich or a pancake into 4 or 8 equal pieces and share them between family members; say each person's fraction.

### 2. `fraction-colour-in` — Colour the Fraction 🎨
- **section**: 7.1
- **summary**: Colour parts of circles, strips and shapes, read the numerator and denominator, and see that all the parts make one whole.
- **book**: Textbook p.118–119 Worked example 1, p.119 Q1, p.123 Check your progress Q1, p.124 Check your progress Q2 · Workbook p.97 Worked example, p.98 Q1–Q2, p.100 Q5, p.101 Q7
- **Learn screens**
  1. *Count the parts.* A round cake (circle) in 5 equal parts. The child counts the slices; the 5 drops into the bottom of the fraction. Bolt: "the denominator says how many equal parts".
  2. *Colour and count.* The child paints 1 part red and 2 blue; the fractions 1/5 and 2/5 build themselves (numerator = parts coloured). The rest is green: 2/5.
  3. *Back to one whole.* The three fractions slide together: 1/5 + 2/5 + 2/5 = 5/5 = 1 whole cake.
- **Practice (10)**
  - Level 1: given shape in 3, 4, 5 or 10 equal parts with some shaded; pick the fraction shaded from 3 options (including a swapped one). Thirds, fifths and tenths only for the denominators taught here; halves and quarters as warm-up.
  - Level 2: instructions "colour 3 green and 5 yellow" on a circle in tenths; type the fraction green, yellow and not coloured (3/10, 5/10, 2/10). Workbook-style grids: shade 3/4 of a 2 × 4 tray of 8 squares, 3/10 of a 2 × 5 tray, or 1/3 of a strip of 9. The tray first splits into 4 (or 3) equal coloured groups, so the child shades whole groups instead of having to divide.
  - Level 3: count the sides of a triangle, pentagon or decagon, cut it into that many equal parts (choose the right cut picture), colour 2 parts and name the fraction (2/3, 2/5, 2/10); a 10-box strip with 3 shaded, then "shade 2 more" and choose 5/10 or its equivalent 1/2 from options.
- **Mistake tags**: `shaded-over-unshaded`, `numerator-denominator-swap`, `not-one-whole`, `miscounted-parts`
- **Parent tips**
  - goal: Name fractions of shapes: the bottom number is how many equal parts, the top number is how many we have.
  - say: "How many equal parts altogether? That's the bottom number." · "How many are coloured? That's the top number." · "If you colour them all, what do you get?"
  - watch: Writing coloured over uncoloured (3/7 instead of 3/10). · Putting the numbers the wrong way up. · Not knowing that 10/10 is one whole. · Workbook pages 98–100 ask for things like 1/3 of 12 squares or 3/4 of 20. That is really division, so help by first splitting the squares into equal groups (by colour or by circling them).
  - away from screen: Colour in a paper plate cut into 10 sections, then say the fraction for each colour.

### 3. `fraction-strip-wall` — Fraction Strip Wall 🧱
- **section**: 7.1
- **summary**: Stack halves, quarters, fifths and tenths strips and drag pieces to find fractions that are the same length (equivalent).
- **book**: Textbook p.120 Q3a–b, p.123 Check your progress Q1b · Workbook p.101 Q6, p.103 Q9
- **Learn screens**
  1. *One whole bar.* A long bar of chocolate is 1 whole. Below it, a bar snapped into 2 halves, then one in 4 quarters. The child drags the quarters under one half: 2 quarters fit exactly. Bolt: "1/2 = 2/4. Different numbers, same amount: equivalent."
  2. *Fifths and tenths.* A fifths bar over a tenths bar: 2 tenths cover 1 fifth, so 1/5 = 2/10.
  3. *Check with a ruler line.* A vertical line slides down the wall to show the edges line up.
- **Practice (8)**
  - Level 1: drag tenths (or quarters) under a fifth (or half) until it is covered; fill 1/2 = __/4 and 1/5 = __/10.
  - Level 2: 2/5 = __/10, 3/5 = __/10, 1/2 = __/10, 8/10 = __/5, 1 whole = __/5 = __/10, 2/4 = __/2 (the workbook needs 1/2 = 5/10 and 8/10 = 4/5, which the textbook strips never show); true or false cards using the wall.
  - Level 3: "Which is bigger?" using the wall (1/5 or 1/10, 3/10 or 1/2) and "shade 2 more boxes": 3/10 becomes 5/10, find it on the wall as 1/2. Workbook-style "same or different?" pairs of pizzas (2/5 and 4/10 the same; 3/4 and 2/3, 1/2 and 1/3, 2/5 and 2/4 not), with an overlay button that lays one on top of the other.
- **Mistake tags**: `different-numbers-different-amount`, `strip-misaligned`, `bigger-denominator-bigger-piece`
- **Parent tips**
  - goal: See that two different fractions can be the same amount (equivalent), like 1/2 and 2/4.
  - say: "Do the ends line up exactly?" · "How many tenths fit under one fifth?" · "Same amount, different name."
  - watch: Thinking 2/4 must be more than 1/2 because the numbers are bigger. · Not lining the strips up at the start.
  - away from screen: Fold two paper strips the same length, one into halves and one into quarters, and lay them next to each other.

### 4. `whole-from-part` — Find the Whole 🔷
- **section**: 7.1
- **summary**: Bolt shows part of a shape and its fraction; the child adds equal pieces to build what the whole could look like.
- **book**: Textbook p.121 Q5, p.121 Q4 (poster showing 1/3 in different shapes) · Workbook p.99 Q4, p.102 Q8
- **Learn screens**
  1. *Same fraction, different shapes.* A one-third poster: 3 triangles, 3 squares, 3 hexagons, one of each shaded. Bolt: one-third means 1 of 3 equal parts, whatever the shape.
  2. *Part to whole.* Two triangles make a diamond and Bolt says "this is 2/4 of a shape". The child drags in 2 more identical triangles that snap edge to edge, making a bigger shape of 4 triangles.
  3. *More than one answer.* Bolt shows two different wholes the child could have made; both are right because each has 4 equal triangles.
- **Practice (8)**
  - Level 1: "this is 1/2 of a shape": add a matching piece (squares, rectangles, triangles); "this is 1/3": add 2 matching pieces.
  - Level 2: "this is 2/4" or "this is 2/3": work out how many more pieces are needed (2 or 1) and build any whole on a triangle or square grid.
  - Level 3: "this is 2/5" or "3/10" of a strip or tile: build the whole; Workbook p.102: an L of 4 squares is one-third, so stamp it 3 times (12 squares), then pretend it is one-fifth and stamp it 5 times (20 squares). Copying the piece builds the whole without any multiplying. Plus "which of these could be the whole?" (choose all correct from 4 pictures, one with a wrong-size piece).
- **Mistake tags**: `wrong-piece-size`, `whole-has-numerator-parts`, `only-one-answer`
- **Parent tips**
  - goal: Work backwards from a part to the whole: the bottom number tells you how many equal pieces the whole needs.
  - say: "If this is 2 of the 4 pieces, how many more pieces do we need?" · "Are your new pieces the same size?" · "Can you make a different whole that still works?"
  - watch: Using pieces of a different size. · Thinking 2/4 means the whole has 2 pieces.
  - away from screen: Use Lego bricks: "These 2 bricks are half of my tower. Build the whole tower."

### 5. `tile-designer` — Tile Designer 🪟
- **section**: 7.1 (Think like a mathematician: Designing tiles)
- **summary**: Colour a square tile along its fold lines to make designs that show a target fraction, then name Bolt's designs.
- **book**: Textbook p.122–123 Think like a mathematician and reflection
- **Learn screens**
  1. *Read a tile.* A bathroom tile split into 4 equal triangles with 2 coloured: Bolt counts parts (4) and coloured (2): 2/4, the same as 1/2. Uncoloured: also 1/2.
  2. *Fold lines.* A square with dotted lines (diagonals, middle lines, a diamond). The child clicks a dotted line to cut along it and sees the equal pieces it makes (2, 4 or 8).
  3. *Many designs, one fraction.* Bolt colours half the tile in three different ways; each is still 1/2.
- **Practice (8)**
  - Level 1: name the fraction shaded and not shaded on Bolt's tiles (halves, quarters).
  - Level 2: "make a tile that is 1/4 shaded" then "3/4", "1/2 in a different way" by clicking pieces; a checker counts the parts.
  - Level 3: tiles split into thirds (3 equal strips) or tenths (2 rows of 5): design one showing 2/3 or 3/10 and name the unshaded fraction; "Bolt says this tile is 1/3 shaded. Is Bolt right?" (some tiles have unequal parts).
- **Mistake tags**: `unequal-parts`, `shaded-over-unshaded`, `missed-equivalent`, `not-checked`
- **Parent tips**
  - goal: Make and read designs that show a fraction, and check that the parts are equal.
  - say: "How many equal pieces is the tile cut into?" · "Is there another way to colour the same fraction?" · "How could you check Bolt's tile?"
  - watch: Counting pieces that are not equal as if they were. · Forgetting to name the part that is not shaded.
  - away from screen: Fold a paper square along its diagonals and middle lines, colour a pattern, and ask a family member to say the fraction coloured.
