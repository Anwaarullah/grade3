**Hidden prerequisites / traps (not in the textbook):**
- **Rotated compasses** (Q3 shows only S at the bottom; **Q6 shows S pointing left**). The textbook always has north at the top. The child must place the other points in clockwise order (N, E, S, W) from wherever one is given. This is the biggest hidden trap in the unit.
- **Relative position between two objects** ("the tent is south of the castle and east of the pond", Q3, Q6, Q8, Q9). The textbook only asks what you see in each direction from one central point.
- **Right angles in tilted shapes** (Q4: a square turned 45 degrees; Q7: tilted angles). The textbook's worked model says a right angle is where a horizontal and a vertical line meet (WB p.219), which is misleading for a tilted square - activities should show that turning a shape does not change its angles.
- **Reflex corners**: the cut-corner square and arrow shapes include corners greater than a right angle and the arrow has inward corners; count only angles inside the shape.
- **Writing two different routes** (Q5) - the child needs to see that order of moves can change but the totals (3 east, 3 north) stay the same.
- No turning/clockwise questions in the workbook at all: the workbook is about right angles and compass position, so turn practice depends on the textbook and the website.

### How the workbook changes the activities

- `right-angle-hunt`: add tilted shapes (square turned 45 degrees, parallelogram, trapezium) and "ring the angles that are not right angles" shapes (pentagon arrow, cut-corner square); a straight-line half turn card (= 2 right angles).
- `compass-garden`: add a level-3 rotated compass (only one letter given, sometimes S on the left or at the bottom) where the child drags the other three letters in clockwise order; add two-reference position sentences ("the shed is north of the pond and west of the gate") on a 3 by 3 picture grid.
- `robot-route`: add a WB Q2-style "place the counters" task (from a star, 4 north, 3 south, 2 west, 1 east) and a "find two different routes, same totals" task on a town grid (school to hospital, 3 east and 3 north).
- `turn-spinner` and `hippo-routes` are textbook-only (no workbook pages); keep them as they are.

### 1. `turn-spinner` - Quarter and Half Turns
- **title:** Quarter and Half Turns
- **icon:** 🔄
- **section:** 15.1
- **summary:** Turn an arrow by quarter, half and full turns, clockwise and anticlockwise, and learn that a quarter turn is a right angle.
- **book:** p.236 Getting started Q1-Q2; p.238 angle diagrams; p.246 Q6; Workbook p.225 Q7c (straight line = half turn)
- **Learn screens:**
  1. *Clockwise.* A clock face with a big arrow pointing to 12. The child clicks "turn clockwise" and the arrow sweeps the way clock hands go. Then anticlockwise. Bolt: "Clockwise goes the same way as the clock hands."
  2. *Quarter turn = right angle.* The arrow turns a quarter (12 to 3). A shaded quarter of the circle and a small square in the corner appear: "This is a right angle." The child turns a quarter the other way (12 to 9).
  3. *Half and full turns.* Two quarter turns make a half turn: the arrow points down and the two arms form a straight line. Two half turns make a full turn: back to the start. The child clicks "half turn" twice and sees it come home.
- **Practice (count 9):**
  - Level 1: start pointing up; click the circle where the arrow will point after a quarter turn clockwise or anticlockwise, or a half turn (4 positions: up, right, down, left).
  - Level 2: start pointing in any of the 4 directions; turns of 1, 2 or 3 right angles clockwise or anticlockwise; click the end position.
  - Level 3: two-step turns ("half turn clockwise, then quarter turn anticlockwise") and reverse questions ("the arrow went from right to left: what turn was it?" - accept half turn either way).
- **Mistake tags:** Turned the wrong way (clockwise mix-up); Turned too far; Did not see half turn = two right angles; Thought a half turn clockwise and anticlockwise end differently.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: know clockwise and anticlockwise, and turn by quarter, half and full turns.
  - Say: "Which way do clock hands go?"; "How many quarter turns make a half turn?"; "Where will it point? Show me with your finger first."
  - Watch for: mixing up clockwise and anticlockwise; thinking a half turn clockwise ends somewhere different from a half turn anticlockwise; counting the starting position as a turn.
  - Away from screen: stand up facing a window; take turns calling "quarter turn clockwise", "half turn" etc. and say what you can see after each turn.

### 2. `right-angle-hunt` - Right Angle Hunt
- **title:** Right Angle Hunt
- **icon:** 📐
- **section:** 15.1
- **summary:** Use a paper right-angle measurer to sort everyday angles and to count right angles inside shapes.
- **book:** p.238; p.239 Q1; p.240 Q2; p.247 Check your progress Q1; Workbook p.218-219 Worked example 1, p.219-220 Q1, p.222 Q4, p.225 Q7
- **Learn screens:**
  1. *Make a measurer.* An animation folds a sheet of paper in half and in half again; the folded corner becomes a draggable right-angle tool.
  2. *Check a corner.* The child drags the measurer onto the corner of a book: it fits exactly (right angle). Onto an open laptop lid: there is a gap (greater). Onto a pair of open scissors: it does not fit inside (less).
  3. *Right angles in shapes.* A rectangle: the child clicks each corner to mark it with a small square (4). Then a right-angled triangle (1).
- **Practice (count 10):**
  - Level 1: one everyday picture with one angle highlighted (door, clock hands at 3 o'clock, slice of pizza, book corner, open fan, roof); drag it into 'right angle', 'greater than' or 'less than'. Measurer available.
  - Level 2: line-drawn angles at different tilts (including tilted right angles and right angles with long or short arms); sort into the three groups.
  - Level 3: count right angles inside a shape (square 4, rectangle 4, right-angled triangle 1, L-shape 5, equilateral triangle 0, regular pentagon 0, star 0, circle 0, irregular hexagon with 2) by clicking corners; type the count.
- **Mistake tags:** Judged by line length; Missed a tilted right angle; Counted the outside corner of an L; Counted a corner twice.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: recognise a right angle and say whether other angles are bigger or smaller.
  - Say: "Does the measurer fit exactly?"; "Is there a gap, or is it squashed?"; "Turn the page - is it still a right angle?"
  - Watch for: thinking a long-armed angle is bigger; not seeing a right angle when it is tilted (the workbook shows a square turned on its corner - still 4 right angles); counting the inward corner of an L-shape as a right angle.
  - Away from screen: fold a paper right-angle measurer together and find four right angles, four bigger and four smaller angles around the house.

### 3. `compass-garden` - Compass Garden
- **title:** Compass Garden
- **icon:** 🧭
- **section:** 15.1
- **summary:** Learn north, south, east and west, and work out which way Bolt faces after turning right angles.
- **book:** p.237 town map; p.241 Q3 a-h; p.248 Check your progress Q2 (label the compass); Workbook p.220 Q2 (label compass), p.221 Q3, p.224 Q6, p.226 Q8, p.227-228 Q9
- **Learn screens:**
  1. *The four points.* A compass rose: the child drags N, E, S, W labels onto it (N at the top first; Bolt teaches "Never Eat Shredded Wheat" going clockwise).
  2. *What can you see?* Bolt stands in the middle of a garden: shed to the north, gate to the east, pond to the south, house to the west. The child clicks a direction button and Bolt turns to face that place.
  3. *Turning by right angles.* Bolt faces west; the child presses "1 right angle clockwise" and Bolt turns to face north. Bolt: "Clockwise from west goes to north."
- **Practice (count 9):**
  - Level 1: "What is to the east?" / "Which direction is the pond?" - click the picture or the compass letter. (Garden places shuffle each round.)
  - Level 2: Bolt faces one direction; turn 1 or 2 right angles clockwise or anticlockwise; click which direction Bolt now faces.
  - Level 3: 3 right angles clockwise or anticlockwise, or two-part turns; also reverse: "Bolt turned from north to west. Which way did he turn?" (quarter anticlockwise or three quarters clockwise).
- **Mistake tags:** Swapped east and west; Turned the wrong way; Counted right angles wrongly; Did not see 3 clockwise = 1 anticlockwise.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: name the four compass directions and use right-angle turns to change direction.
  - Say: "Where is north on this picture?"; "If you turn clockwise from north, where do you face?"; "How many right angles make a half turn?"
  - Watch for: east and west swapped; turning anticlockwise when asked for clockwise; guessing without turning his body (encourage him to stand and turn); being thrown when the compass on the page is turned so north is not at the top (the workbook does this) - go round clockwise N, E, S, W from the letter you know.
  - Away from screen: use a phone compass to find north at home; stick N, E, S, W notes on the walls and play "face east, turn two right angles clockwise".

### 4. `robot-route` - Bolt's Route
- **title:** Bolt's Route
- **icon:** 🤖
- **section:** 15.1
- **summary:** Give Bolt step-by-step instructions on a grid using forward, left and right, or compass directions.
- **book:** p.242 Q4 a-b; p.245 Q5; p.248 Check your progress Q2 (dot-grid moves); Workbook p.220 Q2 (moves from a star), p.223 Q5 (two routes school to hospital)
- **Learn screens:**
  1. *Forward and turns.* Bolt on a grid facing south. The child presses "forward 1", "turn right", "forward 2" and watches. Bolt points out that facing south, his right is west on the map.
  2. *Read a route.* A drawn path on a grid; the child drags instruction cards (forward 1, turn right, forward 4, turn left ...) into order, then presses Go to check Bolt follows the path.
  3. *Compass moves.* A simple grid map of a neighbourhood (home, shop, park, school, bus stop). The child taps N/S/E/W arrows to move a marker from home to the shop; the moves are written as a sentence ("2 north, 3 east").
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: grid map 5 by 4; move from start to a named place with compass arrows; routes need 1 or 2 straight legs (1-4 squares each); write how many squares in each direction.
  - Level 2: follow written compass instructions (e.g. "3 east, 2 south, 1 west") on a dot grid or square grid and click where you finish. Use consistent grids (the finish always fits on the grid).
  - Level 3: build a forward/left/right program for a drawn route with 2-4 turns, starting facing north or south (moves 1-4 squares); then find a different route to the same place.
- **Mistake tags:** Left and right swapped when facing south; Counted the starting square; Mixed compass and turn words; Lost count of squares.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: give and follow clear directions on a grid map using numbers of squares and turns.
  - Say: "Which way is Bolt facing now?"; "Is that his left or your left?"; "How many squares is that?"
  - Watch for: using his own left/right instead of Bolt's; counting the square he starts on as 1; giving instructions without numbers.
  - Away from screen: draw a simple map of your street or home on squared paper; take turns giving directions to a place ("2 squares north, then 3 east") and following them with a toy.

### 5. `hippo-routes` - Hippo to the River
- **title:** Hippo to the River
- **icon:** 🦛
- **section:** 15.1 (Think like a mathematician)
- **summary:** Find all the different routes along paths from a corner to the river, moving only east or south.
- **book:** p.243-244 Think like a mathematician a-h
- **Learn screens:**
  1. *The rules.* A garden-path grid of 1 by 1 blocks; the hippo is top-left, the river bottom-right. The hippo can only walk east or south. The child finds the 2 routes by clicking path segments; each found route is saved as a small picture.
  2. *A bigger grid.* 2 by 2 blocks. Bolt asks: "How many steps east? How many south?" (2 and 2). The child finds routes and the saved pictures show repeats are not counted twice; total 6.
- **Practice (count 8):**
  - Level 1: on a 2 by 2 or 3 by 3 grid, questions like "How many steps east in total?", "Can the hippo ever walk north?", "How many steps east before it has to turn, at most?"
  - Level 2: draw a given route described in words (e.g. "east, east, south, east, south, south") on the grid; or say whether a drawn route is allowed.
  - Level 3: find all routes on a 2 by 3 grid (10 routes) or the 3 by 3 grid (20 routes) by clicking; the counter and thumbnails help; Bolt hints to be systematic (start with all east first).
- **Mistake tags:** Went north or west; Counted the same route twice; Miscounted steps; Stopped before finding all routes.
- **Parent tips:**
  - Goal: follow movement rules and look for all possibilities in an organised way.
  - Say: "Which directions is the hippo allowed to go?"; "Have we found this route before?"; "Can you find them in an order so we don't miss any?"
  - Watch for: random clicking without checking repeats; including routes that go backwards; getting tired on the 3 by 3 (it is a challenge - 20 routes is a lot; stop at 10 and come back later).
  - Away from screen: draw a 2 by 2 block grid on paper and use a toy to walk every route from one corner to the opposite corner, colouring each route a different colour.
