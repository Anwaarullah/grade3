## Proposed activities

### 1. `mirror-line-finder` - Find the fold
- **icon**: 🪞  **section**: 17.1  **book**: p.260 Getting started; p.261 intro and Q1; p.263 Q4; p.265 Q7-Q8; p.266 Q9
- **summary**: Drag and drop a mirror line onto pictures and shapes and fold to test if both halves match.
- **Learn screens**
  1. An apple and a door: Bolt shows a **vertical** line (up and down) through each; the picture folds on the line and the halves match.
  2. An arrow sign and a hamburger: a **horizontal** line (side to side, like the horizon); fold and match.
  3. A rectangle with a diagonal line: the fold does NOT match — "cutting in half is not enough; the halves must be mirror images."
- **Practice (10 questions)**
  - Level 1: everyday pictures (cup, butterfly, house front, leaf, envelope) — click "vertical", "horizontal" or "no line" from a button choice; one line per picture.
  - Level 2: 2D shapes (rectangle, isosceles triangle, right-angled triangle, parallelogram, circle, plus sign, regular pentagon, arrow) — drag a line onto the shape or say "none"; shapes with two lines accept either line.
  - Level 3: tile pictures and square logos with a dashed line already drawn — "is it symmetrical about THIS line?" (including a symmetrical picture with the line in the wrong place); then "how many lines can you find?" for rectangle (2), square (4), plus sign (4), circle (lots).
- **Mistake tags**: `vertical-horizontal-mixup`, `diagonal-halves`, `wrong-line-position`, `outline-only`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Find vertical and horizontal lines of symmetry and check them by folding.
  - Say: "If we fold it on the line, would the two halves sit exactly on top of each other?" / "Is your line going up and down, or side to side?" / "The outside is a square, but what about the picture inside?"
  - Watch for: any line that cuts it in half being called symmetry; mixing up vertical and horizontal.
  - Away from screen: Fold paper cut-outs of a heart, a rectangle and a home-made arrow to find lines that work.

### 2. `reflect-it` - Mirror drawing
- **icon**: 🦋  **section**: 17.1  **book**: p.262 Q2-Q3; p.263 Q3c; p.264 Q6; p.273 Check your progress Q1-Q2
- **summary**: Complete the other half of a picture by reflecting it in a vertical or horizontal mirror line on a square grid.
- **Learn screens**
  1. A half butterfly on a grid with a vertical mirror line. Bolt counts "2 squares from the line here, so 2 squares from the line there" and colours the matching square.
  2. A stepped shape sitting on a horizontal mirror line: the reflection hangs below, flipped. A "flip" animation shows the difference between flipping and sliding.
  3. A shape away from the line (a diamond touching the line at one point; a chevron one square away): count the gap each time.
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: 6×6 grid, vertical line, 3-5 coloured squares touching the line — click squares to reflect.
  - Level 2: 8×8 grid, vertical or horizontal line, 5-8 squares, some 1-3 squares away from the line; two colours.
  - Level 3: outline shapes on dot grid (trapezium, stepped L, zig-zag tree, house pentagon) — click dots to draw the reflected outline; a rectangle 4 by 6 and a triangle with a 7-unit side to be drawn half on each side of the line.
- **Mistake tags**: `slid-not-flipped`, `wrong-distance`, `gap-ignored`, `colour-mismatch`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Draw an accurate reflection of a shape in a mirror line.
  - Say: "How many squares from the line is this corner? So where must its partner go?" / "Should the reflection face the same way or the other way?" / "Check with a mirror, does it look the same?"
  - Watch for: copying the shape the same way round (sliding); reflecting from the shape's edge instead of from the line.
  - Away from screen: Stand a small mirror on a half drawing (half a face, half a house) and draw what you see.

### 3. `six-square-designs` - Symmetry tile designer
- **icon**: 🟨  **section**: 17.1 / 17.2  **book**: p.263 Q5; p.266-267 Think like a mathematician; p.271 Q5-Q6; p.272 Q7 and Think like a mathematician; p.274 Check your progress Q4-Q5
- **summary**: Build designs from squares and pattern blocks with one or two lines of symmetry.
- **Learn screens**
  1. Six square tiles: drag them into a row of 6 — Bolt shows two lines of symmetry. Move one tile up from the end — the lines vanish.
  2. Pattern blocks (hexagons and rhombuses) arranged with a vertical line; the child adds the horizontal line and checks with a fold.
  3. Four-quarter canvas with both lines drawn: colour one quarter and see it reflect into the other three; the colours must match too.
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: given a design with one missing tile, click where it goes to make it symmetrical about the line shown.
  - Level 2: arrange 4-6 square tiles freely; the game reports 0, 1 or 2 lines; target "make one with exactly one line" and "make one with two lines". A gallery stores different finds.
  - Level 3: add shapes to a one-line design so it gains a horizontal line too; colour a two-line picture whose colours are also symmetrical; "straws" mode: build squares with up to 20 sticks (4 per separate square) and make a symmetrical pattern.
- **Mistake tags**: `one-line-only`, `colours-not-matched`, `duplicate-design`, `straw-overcount`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Make patterns that are symmetrical in a vertical line, a horizontal line, or both.
  - Say: "Fold it in your head along this line — does every tile have a partner?" / "Now check the other line." / "Is this a new design or the same one turned round?"
  - Watch for: matching shapes but not colours; counting a turned copy as a new design.
  - Away from screen: Use six sticky notes, toast squares or coasters on the table to make a two-line pattern.

### 4. `pattern-steps` - Grow and shrink patterns
- **icon**: 🔺  **section**: 17.2  **book**: p.268 Worked example 1; p.269-270 Q1-Q4; p.274 Check your progress Q3
- **summary**: Extend or shorten a shape pattern by a constant amount and write the number sentences.
- **Learn screens**
  1. Two triangles (a little tree); click "add 2" to plant another tree each step; number sentences appear: 2, 2 + 2 = 4, 4 + 2 = 6. Bolt points to the "+ 2" every time: "this is the **constant**."
  2. A 4 by 4 tray of 16 cupcakes; click "take 4" to remove one row at a time: 16, 12, 8, 4, 0. The constant is 4 again — taken away this time (**shorten**).
  3. A number track shows the jumps so the child sees the same jump size between each step.
- **Practice (10 questions)**
  - Level 1: constant 1 or 2, start 1-4, up to 4 steps (totals up to 12); click the next picture and type the total.
  - Level 2: constant 2, 3 or 5 starting from the constant, up to 5 steps (totals up to 25); fill missing boxes in number sentences (9 + □ = □); name the constant.
  - Level 3: shorten patterns (16 by 4, 20 by 5, 12 by 3) down to 0; or find the constant from three given terms (3, 6, 9 → 3) and predict the next term; one item asks how many after 3 more steps.
- **Mistake tags**: `added-step-number`, `constant-vs-total`, `wrong-start`, `not-to-zero`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Extend or shorten a pattern by the same amount each time and name that amount.
  - Say: "How many did we add last time? So how many do we add now?" / "What stays the same each step?" / "Count the objects in the picture to check."
  - Watch for: adding 1, 2, 3 … instead of the same number; counting everything from 1 each time instead of counting on.
  - Away from screen: Lay out spoons or blocks in groups of 3 and build three steps; then take groups away.

### 5. `likelihood-line` - How likely?
- **icon**: 📏  **section**: Project 6 (links to Unit 16)  **book**: p.275-276
- **summary**: Place events on a line from "will not happen" to "will happen" and play a guessing game with the parent.
- **Learn screens**
  1. A slider line with "will not happen" on the left and "will happen" on the right. Bolt drags "the sun will rise tomorrow" to the right end and "my cat will go to school by bus" to the left end.
  2. A coin toss: heads goes in the middle — an even chance. "Rain tomorrow" goes somewhere in between; the child is told different people may put it in slightly different places.
  3. The game: Bolt shows a hidden arrow position and gives an event; the child drags an arrow to guess. Close enough (within a marked band) scores a star.
- **Practice (8 questions)**
  - Level 1: place 6 events at an end or in the middle (certain, impossible, coin toss).
  - Level 2: use spinners or bags from Unit 16 (e.g. 3 red, 1 blue): place "pick red" and "pick blue" on the line; nearer the right means more likely.
  - Level 3: Bolt places an arrow; the child chooses which of 3 events fits it best; then parent-and-child turn mode as in the book.
- **Mistake tags**: `might-at-end`, `middle-as-likely`, `reversed-ends`
- **Parent tips**
  - Goal: Show how likely something is by its place on a line, not just by a word.
  - Say: "Is it sure to happen, impossible, or somewhere in between?" / "Is it more likely or less likely than a coin landing on heads?" / "Why did you put it there?"
  - Watch for: "might" events dragged to an end; reading the scale backwards.
  - Away from screen: Draw a line on a long strip of paper and peg up event cards (weather, school, dinner) along it together.

## Verification notes (second pass)
- Second pass re-read every page 260-276 against these notes. The offset in this part of the scan is +2 (not +4 as the brief says).
- 17.1 Q4a is ambiguous in the scan: the green tile picture is itself symmetrical about the centre of its middle column, but the dashed line is drawn on the boundary one column to the right, so it is NOT symmetrical about the line shown (checked on a zoomed crop). The intended answer may be "c only" or "a and c"; the book prints no answer. A build should avoid copying this layout and use clearly correct or clearly wrong lines.
- 17.1 Q5 says each square uses 4 straws, but the example pattern (5 in a row plus 1 on top) has 6 squares, which would need 24 separate straws; it only fits in 20 if shared sides count once (19 straws). Flag to parents.
- 17.1 Q7 pentagon is drawn as a regular pentagon (point up), the arrow is a notched chevron pointing right (horizontal line only).
- The 17.1 reflection question (p.267 top) asks how many different designs there are — referring to the fold-and-cut task on p.266.
- Project 6 is a chance project placed after Unit 17; it continues Unit 16's chance language with a likelihood scale. Marcus's guess in the example matches Sofia's arrow (both at the left end).
