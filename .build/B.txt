ACTIVITY: robot-patrol — "Walk the Edge" (section 6.2, 2D shapes and perimeter). Textbook p.102, p.106 Q6; Workbook p.83 Q2, p.84 Q2c/Q3, p.89 Q9–10.
Learn screens:
1. "What is perimeter?": a garden or playground drawn on a grid (a 5×3 rectangle). Button "Walk the edge": a small walker (e.g. a dot with footsteps or Bolt) moves along the edges; each side lights up with its length as it is completed and chips build the running total 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units (MQ.ui.sum). Perimeter = the distance all the way round.
2. "Edges, not squares": a 3×3 square. Two buttons: "Walk the edge" (highlights the 12 unit edges, counting 1..12) and "Squares inside" (highlights the 9 squares). Bolt: perimeter is the walk round = 12, not 9.
3. "Missing labels": a rectangle labelled only 4 cm (top) and 3 cm (side). The child clicks each unlabelled side to fill in its length (opposite sides are equal), then the sum 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 14 cm appears. lockNext until all filled.
Practice (count 8), number input with the right unit:
- Level 1: rectilinear shapes on a grid (rectangles and L-shapes) — count unit edges; answer in units (or cm if each square is labelled 1 cm). Drawn exactly on the grid.
- Level 2: labelled shapes: triangle 4, 5, 6 cm; irregular pentagon with all sides labelled; rectangle with only two labels (m); regular triangle 4 cm (tick marks show equal sides) = 12 cm; square 5 cm = 20 cm; regular hexagon 3 cm = 18 cm.
- Level 3: regular pentagon with 16 m sides (= 80 m; hint with 16 + 16 + 16 + 16 + 16 or doubling), rectangle 8 m × 7 m (= 30 m), a triangular route 3 km, 4 km, 5 km (= 12 km).
Mistake tags: 'Added only the labelled sides', 'Counted squares inside instead of edges', 'Missed a side'. Reveal: the walker goes round the shape and the full sum appears.

ACTIVITY: regular-irregular — "Shape Sorter" (section 6.2). Textbook p.101, p.104 Q4; Workbook p.82–83 Q1, p.86 Q5, p.89 Q8.
Learn screens:
1. "Regular = ALL sides equal AND ALL angles equal": show an equilateral triangle, square, regular pentagon, regular hexagon with tick marks on sides and angle arcs; then irregular examples (rectangle, right-angled triangle, irregular pentagon). Clicking a shape toggles its side/angle markers.
2. "Tricky shapes": rectangle (angles equal, sides not → irregular), rhombus (sides equal, angles not → irregular), tilted square (still a regular square). The child answers regular/irregular for each of the three, with an explanation after each. lockNext until done.
3. "Draw on dots": a 5×5 dot grid (SVG). Click dots to place 4 corners (lines drawn between them, closing back to the first). When closed, tell the child whether it is a square (regular: 4 equal sides AND equal diagonals — compute exactly with squared distances, works for tilted squares) or an irregular quadrilateral. A Clear button. lockNext until one shape is drawn. Encourage drawing a tilted square.
Practice (count 10): show one SVG shape (some rotated by a random angle) and choices "Regular" / "Irregular". Shape bank: equilateral triangle, isosceles triangle, right-angled triangle, square, tilted square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezium, kite, arrowhead (concave), regular pentagon, irregular pentagon, regular hexagon, irregular hexagon, regular octagon, irregular octagon. Include rectangle, rhombus and a tilted square in every run. Level 3 may also ask "How many sides?" / name the polygon (triangle, quadrilateral, pentagon, hexagon, octagon) for 1–2 questions.
Specific hints: rectangle → 'Are ALL the sides the same length?'; rhombus → 'Look at the corners — are they all the same?'; tilted square → 'Turn your head — it is still a square'. Mistake tags: 'Called a rectangle regular', 'Did not recognise a turned square', 'Called a rhombus regular'. Reveal: draw tick marks on equal sides and angle markers, and explain.

ACTIVITY: perimeter-puzzles — "Perimeter Puzzles" (section 6.2). Textbook p.104 Q3, p.106 Q7, p.107–108; Workbook p.83 Q2b, p.87 Q6, p.90 Q10c.
Learn screens:
1. "Growing squares": squares with sides 1–6 cm on a grid; a "Grow" button adds the next square and fills a table (side | perimeter): 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 cm. Highlight "+4 each time" and say it is the 4 times table: 4, 8, 12, 16 … (each side gets 1 cm longer, 4 sides → 4 cm more). lockNext until all 6 grown.
2. "Working backwards": a square with perimeter 16 cm. Animate 16 dots/tokens being shared out onto the 4 sides one at a time → 4 on each side → each side is 4 cm. Bolt: share the perimeter between 4 equal sides.
3. "Double it": a 3 cm square (perimeter 12 cm); button "Double the sides" → 6 cm square (perimeter 24 cm): the perimeter doubles too; "Halve" goes back.
Practice (count 8):
- Level 1: square with side n (2–6 cm) → perimeter (hint shows n + n + n + n); regular triangle with 4 cm sides → 12 cm.
- Level 2: square with perimeter 8, 12, 16, 20 or 24 cm → side length (hint: share between 4 sides / count back in 4s); "Each side of this 5 cm square gets 2 cm longer. What is the new perimeter?" (= 28 cm; hint: each of the 4 sides grows by 2).
- Level 3: build a rectangle with a given perimeter (12, 16 or 20 units) on a grid by dragging a corner handle (anchored at the top-left; snaps to grid; live perimeter shown); accept ANY rectangle with that perimeter and mention another possible one in the praise. Also a pattern question: shrinking squares 24, 20, 16, … what comes next?
Mistake tags: 'Added only 3 sides', 'Divided by 2 instead of 4', 'Added 2 instead of 8 when each side grew by 2'. Reveal shows the working visually.
